# Inside Out as a memory architecture, mapped to a personal agent memory layer

*Prepared Oct 7, 2026. Personal details from the original version have been removed. Scope: Pixar's* Inside Out *(2015, dir. Pete Docter) and* Inside Out 2 *(2024, dir. Kelsey Mann), mapped to a personal, self-refining memory layer shared by one person's fleet of agents ("the owner" below).*

**Source note.** Film mechanics come from the fan-maintained Inside Out Wiki (Fandom) and its fan transcripts, which I checked against Wikipedia plot summaries and filmmaker interviews. Fan-wiki interpretations are marked **[wiki]**, and inferences of mine are marked **[inference]**. Science claims come from the films' consultants (Keltner, Ekman, Damour, Shohamy) and from memory researchers writing about the films. Section 5 has the full source list.

---

## 1. How the system works in the films

### 1.1 Headquarters and the console
- **Headquarters (HQ)** is the control room of Riley's mind. The emotions stand at a console, and each emotion's input drives Riley's reactions. The console glows in the color of whoever touches it, brighter when the emotion is stronger [wiki: Control Console]. Docter treated Riley as "not a main character but the setting" (Wikipedia, IO1 Production).
- **Who's in charge.** Joy leads in IO1 and tries to keep Sadness's influence small. Each emotion has a protective job: Fear handles physical threats, Disgust social and physical harm, and Anger fairness (Wikipedia cast descriptions).
- **The console grows with Riley.** For baby Riley it's a single button. At the end of IO1, mind worker Fritz installs an "expanded console" with room for the emotions to work as a team, plus a red light marked "PUBERTY" that the emotions decide isn't important yet (IO1 transcript, epilogue; Wikipedia).
- **IO2 Demo Day.** The puberty alarm goes off, and mind workers tear up HQ overnight to make room for new emotions. They leave the job half-finished and remove a gear from the console, so every input now makes Riley overreact. Anger: "I barely touched it." (IO2 transcript; wiki: Control Console). Mann said the construction imagery came from reading about neural pathways being torn down and rebuilt during puberty (Deadline / Laughing Place).
- **Idea bulbs** are plugged into the console to commit Riley to an action. Once one is in, it stays until it expires or another emotion pulls it out. Anger's run-away bulb blacks out the console until Sadness removes it [wiki: Idea Bulb; IO1 plot].

### 1.2 Memory orbs
- Each experience becomes a **glass orb**, colored by the emotion that dominated it: yellow for Joy, blue for Sadness, red for Anger, purple for Fear, green for Disgust. IO2 adds orange for Anxiety, cyan for Envy, indigo for Ennui and pink for Embarrassment. Orbs roll onto HQ shelves during the day [wiki: Memory Orbs; IO1 transcript].
- **Recoloring.** When Sadness touches a happy Minnesota memory, it turns blue. Joy says, "when you touch them, we can't change them back" (IO1 transcript). Keltner and Ekman call this the film's illustration of how "our current emotions shape what we remember of the past" (NYT / Ekman Group).
- **Mixed orbs.** In IO1's climax, Joy and Sadness press the console together and create a half-gold, half-blue core memory. A year later, Riley's core memories are mostly multicolored, mixed-emotion orbs (IO1 transcript; Wikipedia plot).
- Factual memories are shown as gray, with no emotion color [wiki: Memory Orbs, uncertain].

### 1.3 Short-term to long-term memory, forgetting, and the dump
- **Daytime buffer.** New orbs sit on HQ shelves.
- **Nightly flush.** When Riley sleeps, the emotion on "Dream Duty" presses the end-of-day memory flush. Orbs travel through floor tracks and a ceiling recall tube and "rain down" into Long Term Memory (IO1 transcript: "Let's get those memories down to Long Term"; "Looks like we're going into REM... I'll take care of sending these to Long Term").
- **Long Term Memory** is a huge maze of curving shelves, designed to evoke the brain's folds. It's easy to get lost in, and in IO2 it has grown so much that Joy can't find her way [wiki: Long Term Memory].
- **Recall tubes** run in both directions. Emotions can pull a specific memory up to HQ's screen, and mind workers sometimes send random ones, like the TripleDent Gum jingle that "plays in Riley's head over and over" [wiki: Recall Tube; IO1 transcript].
- **Forgetters** are mind workers who vacuum up faded memories: "When Riley doesn't care about a memory, it fades." They curate rather than wipe. On piano lessons: "Save 'Chopsticks' and 'Heart and Soul', get rid of the rest." On U.S. presidents: keep Washington, Lincoln, and one more (IO1 transcript, "Forgetters" scene).
- **The Memory Dump** is a chasm below the islands. Faded memories pile up there, crumble to dust, and are "forgotten forever." A Forgetter says, "Nothing comes back from the dump." (IO1 transcript).
- **Bing Bong** is Riley's toddler-era imaginary friend, part cotton candy, part elephant, part cat. He wanders Long Term Memory because Riley no longer thinks about him. He sacrifices himself in the Dump so Joy can escape and fades away (IO1 plot). Richard Kind described the character as "the fading of childhood" (Wikipedia, Writing).

### 1.4 Core memories and Islands of Personality
- **Core memories** come from "super important" moments. They sit in a holder at the center of HQ and beam light to an **Island of Personality**. Riley's five original islands are Family, Friendship, Hockey, Honesty and Goofball (IO1 transcript; wiki: Core Memories).
- **Collapse.** When Joy and Sadness are sucked out with the core memories, the islands go dark. Each one collapses into the Dump after Riley acts against it: Goofball when she won't laugh with Dad, Friendship when she snaps at Meg, Hockey when she quits, Honesty when she steals the credit card, and Family last and slowest, as she runs away [wiki: Islands of Personality; IO1 plot].
- **Fear's failed substitute.** Fear recalls "every hockey memory I can think of" to stand in for the missing core memory. The holder ejects them and Hockey Island falls anyway (IO1 transcript).
- **Rebuild.** The mixed joy-and-sadness memory creates a new, larger Family Island with the Golden Gate Bridge on it. A year later there are about ten islands. The old ones are rebuilt and new ones appear: Tragic Vampire Romance, Boy Band, Fashion, and others (IO1 epilogue).
- **Islands change over time.** By IO2, "Boy Band Island finally broke up," Friendship Island has grown and blocks the view of a smaller Family Island, and Honesty Island disappears while Riley is being dishonest (IO2 transcript; [wiki]).

### 1.5 Other regions of the mind
- **Train of Thought** carries crates of daydreams, facts and opinions between regions and stops while Riley sleeps. When Joy spills the **Facts and Opinions** crates, Bing Bong says people mix them up all the time and puts them all in the FACTS crate (IO1 transcript).
- **Dream Productions** is a studio that films each night's dreams from the day's material, using a "reality distortion filter" (IO1 transcript). The Conversation notes that dreams being built from distorted pieces of the day loosely matches consolidation research (Talarico).
- **Imagination Land** is a theme park (French Fry Forest, Cloud Town, the Imaginary Boyfriend Generator) that is being demolished and rebuilt as Riley grows. In IO2, Anxiety puts its workers to work generating worst-case futures: "They're using Riley's imagination against her." (IO2 transcript; wiki).
- **Abstract Thought** is a shortcut where visitors get abstracted in four stages: "non-objective fragmentation," then deconstruction, then two-dimensional, then non-figurative (IO1 transcript).
- **The Subconscious** is a locked cavern with guards, holding Riley's deepest fears (Jangles the Clown, Grandma's vacuum, the basement stairs) and "troublemakers" (IO1; wiki).

### 1.6 IO2: the Belief System and the Sense of Self
- **Memories form beliefs.** Joy narrates: "her islands aren't the only things made by memories. Way down at the root level, these memories were also creating beliefs." Beliefs are glowing strings in a pool fed by the **Stream of Consciousness**. Pluck a string and you hear the belief in Riley's own voice: "I'm kind," "Mom and Dad are proud of me" (IO2 transcript). Mann said he chose voice and waveforms so the audience would care about each belief, with a clean waveform for a healthy belief and a chaotic one for a distorted belief (Deadline; Empire).
- **Beliefs form a Sense of Self.** Joy: "when you put all those beliefs together, they make... her Sense of Self." It's a crystal on a pedestal in HQ, where the core memory holder used to be, and "it's what helps Riley make good choices." Its main belief at the start is "I'm a good person" (IO2 transcript). The wiki says the Sense of Self can override an emotion's push, citing Riley's hesitation when Anxiety pushes her to abandon her friends [wiki, interpretive].
- **Mann on what it means:** "You're being raised by other people and taking on their beliefs... suddenly you go: Wait. What do I believe?... all of those memories feed those beliefs and create, kind of collectively, how you see yourself." He called it the hardest design in the film (IndieWire).
- **Joy's "Riley Protection System"** is a spring-loaded launcher. Joy: "This is for all those memories that belong in the back of the mind... A one-way expressway to 'We're not gonna think about that right now'... We keep the best and toss the rest!" The emotions run a nightly sweep, flinging out embarrassing memories (waving at the wrong person, forgetting a name) and the penalty memory (IO2 transcript).
- **The Back of the Mind** is a far wasteland where those memories pile into a mountain. Joy also launches the puberty alarm there (IO2 transcript; wiki).
- **Anxiety's replacement self.** Anxiety: "I plan for the future... my team has run all the data and we're looking at the following likely scenarios." She rips out the Sense of Self, launches it to the Back of the Mind, bottles up the original emotions in the **Vault of Secrets**, and plants memories she picks herself to grow a new self. The first belief is "If I'm a Fire Hawk, I won't be alone," then "If I'm good at hockey, I'll have friends." The finished self says **"I'm not good enough"** (IO2 transcript). The wiki says emotions "aren't able to have control on what belief is created from a memory": Anxiety tries to make Riley better and makes her feel inadequate [wiki, interpretive]. Riley overplays in the final match, crashes into Grace, and has a panic attack (Wikipedia plot).
- **Resolution.** Joy's team blows up the mountain of suppressed memories and rides the avalanche into the Belief System. The bad memories form bad beliefs ("Wait... no!"). Putting the old Sense of Self back doesn't work. Anxiety: "WE don't get to choose who Riley is." Joy pulls the old self out and lets a third one grow from all the memories: "I'm selfish. I'm kind. I'm not good enough! I'm a good person. I need to fit in. But I want to be myself. I'm brave. But I get scared." Joy's last line: "we love ALL of our girl. Every messy, BEAUTIFUL piece of her." (IO2 transcript).
- **New emotions.** Anxiety (catastrophizes in order to avert bad outcomes), Envy (wants what others have), Ennui (boredom and sarcasm; she opens a "Sar-chasm"), Embarrassment, and Nostalgia, who is told to wait "ten years, two graduations, and a best friend's wedding" before her turn at the console (Wikipedia cast; [wiki: Nostalgia]). Mann's first draft had nine new emotions. Schadenfreude, Jealousy and Guilt were cut, and Shame was dropped partly because Keltner doesn't treat it as a distinct primary emotion (Wikipedia, IO2 Development).
- **The Vault of Secrets** holds things Riley hides: an embarrassing childhood TV show, a video-game crush, and a "Deep Dark Secret" who won't come out because "it's not time yet" [wiki: Vault of Secrets].

### 1.7 What the science says is accurate and what's fiction

| Film mechanism | Verdict | Who says so |
|---|---|---|
| Emotions organize thought, perception and memory rather than disrupting them | **Accurate** (the film's central thesis) | Keltner & Ekman, NYT 2015 |
| Recalling a memory changes it (Sadness recoloring orbs) | **Accurate** in spirit: reconsolidation, "you bring a memory back to life, and that will change the way it's re-stored" | Shohamy (a consultant), Vulture; Talarico, The Conversation |
| Memories mostly drift *more negative* | **Partly.** More often memories drift *more positive* (the "Pollyanna principle") | Talarico |
| Sleep moves the day into long-term memory, and dreams remix the day | **Broadly accurate.** Sleep consolidation is active, and it turns memories into abstracted, gist-like representations | Talarico; Klinzing et al., *Nature Neuroscience* 2019; Harvard Medicine Magazine |
| Memories are intact orbs on shelves | **Fiction.** Memory is distributed and rebuilt on recall, "less like the public library... more like Wikipedia with its many linked pages" | Talarico; Shohamy |
| Faded memories go to a dump and disappear forever | **Fiction (decay theory).** Forgetting is mostly retrieval failure: traces remain and the right cue brings them back | Talarico; Wixted in Time/THR ("memory is malleable") |
| Five "core memories" drive personality | **Made up.** The concept is not scientific. Traits are relatively stable, self-defining memories are examples of larger patterns, and you can't predict which events become important | Mann ("We made that up!", Screen Rant); Van Bergen & Harris, The Conversation 2022; Wikipedia |
| Emotional events stick better | **Accurate** | Shohamy; Van Bergen & Harris |
| Anxiety is protective but can tip into dysfunction or panic | **Accurate.** It is pathological only when it anticipates threats that aren't real or reacts out of proportion | Damour (NPR, Science Friday) |
| A healthy self holds shortcomings alongside self-regard | **Accurate.** The clinical goal is "to help people acknowledge their shortcomings while still hold[ing] themselves at a reasonable level of self-regard" (Keltner quoting Nancy McWilliams) | Keltner, Berkeley Talks 2024 |
| A sense of self is a narrative made of beliefs | **Partial.** There is also an experiential, moment-to-moment self | Psychology Today (Pacific Heart blog) |
| The roster of emotions | **Simplification.** Keltner's lab works with roughly 25 emotions that blend and change | Keltner, Berkeley Talks |

## 2. Mapping table: film mechanism → agent memory

How to read it: **Riley = the owner, the setting.** The **emotions at the console** are the agents replying in the moment, each with its own bias. **Memory orbs** are signals, and **orb color** is the signal's type and valence. **Mind workers** are background jobs. **Long Term Memory** is the raw signal log. The **Belief System** is the belief notes, and the **Sense of Self** is the compiled worldview each agent reads.

| Film mechanism | Equivalent in the agent system | Design lesson | Covered by the first sketch? |
|---|---|---|---|
| HQ console, where emotions co-pilot | The agent's reply-time context (prompt plus retrieved notes) | Memory only matters if it reaches the console when the agent decides. His "read core + segment hub before replying" is right. | Yes |
| Console glows by emotion and intensity | Signal metadata: type and **heat** (how intense the pushback was) | Record how strongly he pushed back, not only what he said. Heat should also slow promotion (see Anxiety below). | Partly (type yes, heat no) |
| Expanded console at age 12 | The read budget grows as segments mature | More beliefs per read as a segment matures, still capped | Partly (page caps) |
| Puberty alarm and Demo Day: half-rebuilt HQ, overreacting console | A life-phase pivot, e.g. a change of career direction | A pivot is a declared **phase-change event**. Mark affected beliefs "needs reconfirmation", and check that the system isn't overreacting to small new signals. | **Missing** |
| Idea bulb that locks in an action | A standing instruction held for one task or session | Session-scoped commitments expire. Don't promote them to beliefs automatically. | Implicit |
| Memory orb created per experience | One signal per pushback, append-only | Capture the exchange verbatim (bot output plus his reply) so later recoloring can't rewrite it | Yes |
| Orb color = emotion | Signal type ("too generic", "voice remixed", "wrong fact", "over-anchoring") and valence | **Joy orbs matter too.** Log what landed (accepted unchanged, "yes, that"), not only complaints. A memory built only from pushback is an Anxiety-built self. | **Missing (positive signals)** |
| Sadness recoloring memories on recall (reconsolidation) | A belief is updated whenever it's used and then accepted or pushed back on | Beliefs should update when they're used, but the current mood mustn't rewrite history. Recolor beliefs, never raw signals, and keep version history. | Partly |
| Mixed-color core memories at the end of IO1 | Beliefs with conditions or tension ("terse in chat, detailed in specs") | Allow paired, conditional beliefs (`applies_when`, `except_when`, `tension_with`) instead of settling every contradiction by picking one side | **Missing as a schema field** |
| HQ shelves → nightly flush → Long Term | Session buffer → nightly distiller → log | Matches his nightly consolidation. Consolidation should produce the **gist** (abstraction), as sleep research describes. | Yes |
| Train of Thought stops while Riley sleeps | Don't distill while a session is live | Run distillation on a finished day, not mid-conversation | Yes (nightly) |
| Long Term Memory maze; Joy gets lost | A large signal log | You need maps: hub notes, indexes, backlinks. Agents should never browse the raw log. | Yes (hubs) |
| Recall tube and random recall (the TripleDent jingle) | Retrieval at reply time | **Earworm risk.** A belief or phrase pulled up often isn't necessarily important. This is the "bots keep reusing the same signature line" failure. Track reuse, penalize it, and never quote him verbatim. | **Missing** |
| Forgetters: "Save Chopsticks and Heart and Soul, get rid of the rest" | Decay plus compression job | Forgetting should **compress**: shrink a cluster of signals down to a few exemplars rather than delete them | Partly (decay) |
| Memory Dump: "Nothing comes back from the dump" | Deleted or decayed beliefs | Science says forgetting is retrieval failure. Use a **cold archive** that a cue can bring back, not deletion. | **Change** |
| Bing Bong fading | An outdated belief or identity from an earlier phase | Let outdated selves fade gracefully, but keep them as history ("as of Sep: previous goal") | Partly |
| Core memory → Island of Personality | Pinned belief → segment hub | Pin rarely. In the film one event instantly builds an island, which is the over-anchoring he wants to avoid. Keep "explicit 'always or never' = immediate rule" narrow and still reviewable. | Yes, but tighten |
| Island collapses when behavior contradicts it | Contradicting evidence weakens a belief | Contradicting signals should **weaken** a belief (negative reinforcement), not only age it out | **Missing** (decay is time-based only) |
| Fear stacks hockey memories in place of a core memory | Promoting a belief because many weak, similar signals pile up | Count isn't validity. Require variety (different bots, days, contexts) plus his approval. | Yes (his pattern rule) |
| New islands; Boy Band Island "broke up"; Friendship outgrows Family | Segment lifecycle | Segments are born, grow, go dormant and retire. Weight hubs by recent activity while core persists. | **Missing** |
| Facts and Opinions crates mixed up | Biographical facts vs. preference beliefs | **Two stores**: facts (sourced, single owner, true or false) and beliefs (patterns, approved, contextual). Never file opinions as facts. | **Missing** (fact drift between bots) |
| Dream Productions: day's material plus reality distortion | Distiller hypotheses and cross-segment remixes | Put speculative patterns in a **"dreams" note** that agents never read until he approves | **Missing** |
| Imagination Land, renovated as Riley grows | Ideation / prototyping segment | Prototype ideas and preferences change fast. Use shorter decay in exploratory segments. | Partly |
| Abstract Thought: abstraction until flat and two-dimensional | Over-distilled beliefs ("be less generic") | Each belief needs a concrete example and a counterexample, or it's too abstract to steer anything | **Missing** |
| Subconscious: locked fears and troublemakers | Known triggers (what makes him angry) | Keep triggers as a guarded "do not do" list that agents check, not a list they recite | Partly |
| Vault of Secrets; Deep Dark Secret "not time yet" | Sensitive or private material | An access-controlled note class that agents don't read by default | **Missing** |
| Belief System: strings spoken in Riley's voice, each tied to the memory that grew it | Belief notes with backlinks to signals | Write beliefs as short first-person claims he can recognize ("I want options, not finished decisions") with evidence links. They help his review, and agents apply them without quoting. | Yes (backlinks) |
| Sense of Self crystal: consulted when choosing, can override an emotion | A compiled per-segment self-portrait, used as the pre-reply check | Generate a short **Sense-of-Self note** per segment each night from approved beliefs, contradictions kept. Show him the diff. | **Missing as an artifact** |
| Joy's Riley Protection System and the Back of the Mind | Rejected or uncomfortable proposals and signals | **Don't let approval become Joy's launcher.** Use "not now" (parked, resurfaces later), not "rejected → gone". Approve for *accuracy*, not flattery. | **Missing** |
| Anxiety's replacement self ("I'm not good enough") | Over-fitting to a recent burst of negative signals | **Change budget and cooldown**: limit belief changes per night, delay high-heat sessions one cycle, apply changes as diffs, never auto-replace the core | **Missing** |
| Anxiety's worst-case futures from Imagination | Agents over-predicting what he'll want | Anticipation is useful (Damour), but don't act on imagined preferences with no signal behind them | Implicit |
| Third Sense of Self: "WE don't get to choose who Riley is" | The worldview emerges from signals | The distiller reports what the signals show and doesn't steer toward a nicer belief. His gate checks accuracy. | Partly |
| Nostalgia told to wait years for her turn | Dormant signal types | Some signals belong to later phases. Keep them dormant rather than delete them. | n/a |
| New emotions in IO2; about 25 in Keltner's research | A taxonomy of signal types | Keep the signal-type list **extensible**. New pushback categories will appear as his needs mature. | **Missing** |
| Sadness's real job: signal that help is needed | Pushback as a call for clarification | Sometimes a pushback means "ask me", not "write a rule". The distiller can propose a *question* instead of a belief. | Partly (conflict → question) |

## 3. Design lessons and additions to the architecture

### What the first sketch already has right
1. **Nightly consolidation** (the HQ flush → Long Term). This matches sleep research: consolidation produces gist and integrates new material with what's already known.
2. **Raw signals kept separate from distilled beliefs**, with backlinks (orb → string → self).
3. **Patterns over one-offs.** This avoids the film's least scientific idea, that one event instantly creates a personality island.
4. **Reading only core plus the segment hub** (the console only sees what's recalled).

### Additions, in priority order
1. **Anti-Anxiety guardrails (highest value).** IO2 is a case study of a self rewritten by one emotion during a high-stress stretch, using memories it picked itself. Add:
   - `heat` on every signal; signals from high-heat sessions wait one distill cycle before they can change anything;
   - a **change budget** (for example, at most 3 belief changes per segment per night);
   - changes always applied as diffs; core beliefs never auto-replaced;
   - a check on recent signals: if more than X% of tonight's evidence comes from one session or bot, flag it for him instead of promoting it.
2. **Positive signals ("Joy orbs").** Log what landed: outputs he used unchanged, "yes, exactly," a quick thumbs-up. Without them, the worldview is a list of prohibitions. That's the anxious self ("I'm not good enough"), and it pushes agents toward hedged, generic output.
3. **No Riley Protection System.** Replace "rejected" with **"not now / parked."** Parked proposals and uncomfortable signals (times he contradicted himself, times the agent was right) resurface on a schedule. The approval question is "*is this accurate?*", not "*do I like this?*" IO2's resolution only works because the suppressed memories come back.
4. **Mixed beliefs as a first-class schema.** Add `applies_when`, `except_when`, `tension_with:[[other belief]]` and `examples` / `counterexamples` to the frontmatter. The healthy self is "I'm brave, but I get scared," not a single tidy rule.
5. **Sense-of-Self compiled note per segment.** Each night, regenerate a short (about 150-word) self-portrait per segment plus core from approved beliefs, contradictions intact. Agents read that, then follow links only as needed. Show him the nightly diff so drift is visible early, the way the Sense of Self's color changes in the film.
6. **Archive, don't dump.** Decay moves beliefs to `status: archived`, still indexed and revivable when a matching signal shows up. Nothing is hard-deleted.
7. **Facts vs. opinions, two stores.** Biographical facts (dates, ownership, affiliations) live in a sourced facts store with one owner. Preferences and beliefs live in the belief graph. This addresses drift where bots misremember project dates or who owned what.
8. **Earworm guard.** Track how often each belief or phrase appears in agent outputs. Above a threshold, reduce how often it's retrieved and forbid verbatim reuse. This targets the overused-signature-line failure directly.
9. **Contradicting evidence weakens beliefs**, not only time. An island collapses when Riley acts against it, so a belief should lose strength when he pushes back on it.
10. **Phase-change event (the puberty alarm).** When he declares a pivot, mark affected beliefs `needs_reconfirm` and raise the promotion threshold for a while, so the half-rebuilt system doesn't overreact.
11. **Segment lifecycle:** `forming → active → dormant → retired`, with hub weight following activity (Boy Band Island broke up; Friendship outgrew Family).
12. **"Dreams" note for hypotheses.** The distiller's speculative cross-segment patterns go into a quarantined note. Agents never read it until he promotes an item from it.
13. **Concreteness rule (the Abstract Thought trap).** A belief without at least one concrete example and one counterexample isn't eligible for promotion.
14. **Vault class** for sensitive notes, excluded from default agent reads.
15. **Extensible signal taxonomy.** Start small (Inside Out started with 5, out of Docter's 27 candidates) and let the distiller propose new signal types.

### A compact flow with the additions
```
pushback / acceptance → signal{type, valence, heat, bot, segment, verbatim}
  → nightly distiller (after cooldown) → {strengthen | weaken | propose | question | dream}
  → change budget + recency/heat check
  → owner reviews: accurate? → approve | edit | not-now(parked)
  → belief graph (facts store separate) → per-segment Sense-of-Self note (+ diff)
  → agents read Sense-of-Self + linked beliefs → reply → reuse tracked (earworm guard)
  → decay / contradiction → archived (revivable), never deleted
```

## 4. Where the analogy breaks
1. **One mind vs. many separate agents.** Riley's emotions share one HQ and see the same screen in real time. The owner's bots don't share context unless something is written down, so "shared HQ" has to be built (files plus the compiled self-notes), and keeping them in sync is a real engineering cost the film never has.
2. **Recordings vs. lossy summaries.** Orbs replay events perfectly. Agents paraphrase and misattribute, which is a bigger risk than Sadness recoloring. That's why signals should be captured verbatim and immutable. (Real human memory is reconstructive too, so the film is wrong in a different direction.)
3. **Riley can't edit her mind; the owner can.** He's both Riley and a mind worker. That's a strength (he can approve directly), but it means he can play Joy: curating a flattering self and launching the inconvenient parts to the back of the mind. The IO2 lesson applies to the approver as much as to the agents.
4. **"Nobody chooses who Riley is" vs. an approval gate.** The films say the self emerges and that controlling it backfires. His design deliberately adds a chooser. They reconcile only if approval checks *accuracy and scope*, not *desirability*.
5. **Forgetting.** The Dump deletes permanently. Science says forgetting is mostly retrieval failure, and files cost nothing to keep. Copy the Forgetters' curation, not the Dump.
6. **Core memories → personality.** The films' central mechanism is, by Mann's own admission, made up. Researchers say traits are fairly stable and self-defining memories are examples of patterns. Many pushbacks are about the output, not about who he is, so the distiller has to separate "fix this draft" from "this is how I think".
7. **Who the emotions are.** "Emotion" maps to two things in his system: the bots (actors at the console) and the signal types (orb colors). Mixing them up leads to design mistakes. One sensible split: bots are console operators, signal types are colors, background jobs are mind workers.
8. **No adversarial inputs in Riley's head.** His system takes writes from many bots and from external content, so hallucinated facts or injected text can become "memories". Riley's mind has nothing like that, so provenance and source fields need more rigor than the film suggests.
9. **Timescale and scale.** The films compress weeks into days and give each island one memory. His beliefs will need gradual, statistical evidence over weeks, plus explicit decay settings the film never shows.
10. **Visual metaphor vs. retrieval.** Shelves and orbs suggest browsing. Agents actually retrieve by search or links within a context budget. Even the films' own consultants prefer the Wikipedia-of-linked-pages picture, which happens to match Obsidian.
